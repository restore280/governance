## Resolution 1: Domain Identification and Scoring

The Board hereby adopts the following identification of six candidate Domains under Section 4 of the Policy, together with their Importance and Urgency scores under Section 5:

| Domain | Description | Importance | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fundraising | Determining effective means to raise funds toward the Corporation's financial independence | 8 | 6 |
| Outreach | Creating online presence raising awareness of the Corporation, its mission, and aligned causes | 7 | 8 |
| Lobbying | Impacting political discourse within Section 501(h) limits and exploring other aligned political organizing | 4 | 7 |
| Research | Conducting novel research and meta-analyses in furtherance of the Corporation's mission | 6 | 8 |
| Deployment | Direct deployment of physical infrastructure | 2 | 9 |
| Capital & Partnership Facilitation | Facilitating third-party capital toward opportunities built by others | 1 | 4 |

Pursuant to Section 5.2 of the Policy and Bylaws Section 9.6, the completed mission-alignment review for each Domain, serving as the documented basis for its Importance score, and the documented basis for each Domain's Urgency score under Section 5.3, are as follows:

### Fundraising

(a) Condition: funding concentration risk, the concern addressed in Bylaws Section 9.7, bears on both the Corporation's ability to carry out its exempt purposes and its independence. (b) Failure: nonprofit funding models generally depend on donor relationships that can create the kind of concentration risk Section 9.7 identifies. (c) Evidence: nonprofits remain the most broadly trusted institutional sector (56% high trust), and joint public advocacy specifically raises trust in the nonprofit doing it. (d) Leverage: this domain's role is to determine and validate an effective resourcing approach before committing significant resources to any single method. (e) Why restore280: establishing a resourcing approach consistent with the independence standard in Bylaws Section 9.7 is foundational to pursuing any other domain. (f) Outcome: a validated, resourced approach to funding, not funds raised itself. (g) Metrics: cost-per-dollar-raised, conversion, and retention, once a specific approach is selected. (h) Harms: solicitation risk to the appearance of mission capture; resourcing this domain trades against other domains' capacity. (i) Risky assumption: that general nonprofit trust transfers to a new organization with no track record. (j) Revise trigger: no viable approach within this domain proves cost-effective relative to the time and capital committed to it.

**Urgency basis:** nonprofits are the most trusted institutional sector measured (56% high trust); joint advocacy specifically raises trust in the nonprofit doing it; national donor participation has declined despite steady sector-wide trust.

### Outreach

(a) Condition: the Corporation's exempt purposes include public education directed toward restoring the full range of conditions human dignity requires, per the Certificate of Incorporation, and that education depends on reaching an audience. (b) Failure: institutional capacity for public education on the conditions named in the Corporation's purposes, atmospheric stability, housing affordability, and governance accountability, has generally declined. (c) Evidence: independent creators now beat national outlets on trusted reporting (34% vs. 12%), and social media is closing on TV as primary news source, concentrated among younger viewers. (d) Leverage: public-facing communication is the one domain restore280 can execute unilaterally, with no third-party cooperation needed. (e) Why restore280: an organization whose exempt purpose is public education is well positioned to occupy this domain directly. (f) Outcome: an engaged audience with measurably improved understanding of the conditions named in the Corporation's purposes. (g) Metrics: audience engagement and growth over time. (h) Harms: engagement-optimized incentives can erode educational rigor; time cost against other domains. (i) Risky assumption: that audience trust translates to durable retention, not one-off engagement. (j) Revise trigger: engagement metrics fail to show measurable audience growth over a defined period.

**Urgency basis:** independent/online creators beat national outlets on trusted reporting (34% vs. 12%); social media is closing on TV as Americans' primary news source, driven by younger audiences.

### Lobbying

(a) Condition: the Corporation's exempt purposes include nonpartisan advocacy, per the Certificate of Incorporation, directed at the governance-accountability systems the Corporation's purposes address. (b) Failure: policy change on these systems is otherwise constrained by ordinary political incentives. (c) Evidence: lobbying itself polls hostile when corporate (63% oppose direct corporate donations to candidates), but nonprofit joint advocacy raises trust in the nonprofit doing it. (d) Leverage: the Corporation's Section 501(h) election, referenced in the Editorial and Compliance Policy, gives defined expenditure room instead of an ambiguous substantiality standard. (e) Why restore280: nonpartisan advocacy is already named among the Corporation's exempt purposes. (f) Outcome: measurable policy-relevant interventions within defined expenditure limits. (g) Metrics: interventions conducted, expenditure against 501(h) limits. (h) Harms: expenditure draws from the same pool as other domains; activity in this domain must remain nonpartisan, consistent with the Corporation's exempt purposes. (i) Risky assumption: that credibility built in other domains transfers to tolerance for this domain. (j) Revise trigger: expenditure approaches either 501(h) limit.

**Urgency basis:** public opinion on lobbying skews hostile toward direct corporate money in politics (63% opposed), but nonprofit-led joint advocacy specifically raises trust in the nonprofit doing it.

### Research

(a) Condition: the Corporation's exempt purposes include research directed toward restoring the full range of conditions human dignity requires, per the Certificate of Incorporation. (b) Failure: original research on the conditions named in the Corporation's purposes is not otherwise being produced at the scale the underlying problem requires. (c) Evidence: scientists remain the single most-trusted professional group Pew measures (77% confidence); independent creators beat national outlets on trusted reporting, suggesting rigor paired with a trusted distribution channel outperforms either alone. (d) Leverage: original analysis strengthens the credibility of the Corporation's broader public-facing work. (e) Why restore280: research is already named among the Corporation's exempt purposes. (f) Outcome: analysis whose claims are independently defensible. (g) Metrics: claim density and defensibility, downstream citation or engagement by researchers or educators. (h) Harms: an error in this domain is more visible and requires faster correction than an academic-style process allows; time pressure from other domains could compress research discipline. (i) Risky assumption: that this domain's output carries the same evidentiary discipline without a dedicated process forcing that rigor. (j) Revise trigger: correction rate on this domain's claims exceeds a defined threshold.

**Urgency basis:** scientists remain the single most-trusted professional group measured (77% confidence); 84% of Americans still call federal science investment worthwhile even amid funding cuts.

### Deployment

(a) Condition: the Corporation's purposes address atmospheric stability directly, per the Certificate of Incorporation, and physical infrastructure is one means by which atmospheric conditions are restored. (b) Failure: policy and private capital generally underfund this kind of infrastructure relative to its impact. (c) Evidence: 2026 US polling shows 78% support for renewable energy research funding and 77% support for CO2 regulation. (d) Leverage: direct capital deployment sidesteps the multi-year lag of policy-driven infrastructure funding. (e) Why restore280: the weakest link in this chain. No demonstrated execution track record in this domain, as distinct from facilitating third-party capital toward it, which is a separate domain. (f) Outcome: infrastructure built or substantially funded, with documented impact per dollar. (g) Metrics: impact per dollar, on-schedule delivery. (h) Harms: private-benefit and excess-benefit-transaction risk under IRC §4958; the Capital Deployment and Partnership Policy already disqualifies any opportunity lacking a clear causal mechanism to an exempt-purpose outcome, and that mechanism does not yet exist generally for this domain. (i) Risky assumption: that an organization with no track record in this domain can execute credibly. (j) Revise trigger: no opportunity within this domain clears the Capital Deployment and Partnership Policy's disqualifying-factors test.

**Urgency basis:** 2026 U.S. polling shows 78% support for renewable energy research funding and 77% support for regulating CO2 as a pollutant.

### Capital & Partnership Facilitation

(a) Condition: the Corporation's exempt purposes include capital facilitation directed toward restoring the full range of conditions human dignity requires, per the Certificate of Incorporation. (b) Failure: no actor is currently positioned to evaluate capital deployment purely against that purpose, independent of other incentives. (c) Evidence: individual investor interest in sustainable/impact investing hit 92% in 2026, up from 88%, alongside a values-behavior gap (56% say they care about ESG, only 39% want it acted on). (d) Leverage: this domain is lower-capital-intensity for restore280 than building or fully funding infrastructure itself. (e) Why restore280: capital facilitation is already named among the Corporation's exempt purposes, and an organization not itself dependent on the capital it evaluates has less incentive conflict, consistent with the independence concern in Bylaws Section 9.7. (f) Outcome: documented capital-to-opportunity matches with tracked outcomes. (g) Metrics: dollars facilitated, outcomes documented per the Capital Deployment and Partnership Policy's evaluation criteria. (h) Harms: reputational exposure if a facilitated partner underperforms; disqualifying factors need a first real case to validate the intake process screens for them. (i) Risky assumption: that the Corporation can build real underwriting capability without dedicated capital-markets expertise. (j) Revise trigger: the first facilitated match doesn't materialize, or the partner fails basic outcome documentation.

**Urgency basis:** individual investor interest in sustainable/impact investing reached 92% in 2026, up from 88%, alongside a values-behavior gap between stated concern and desired action.

## Resolution 2: Eligibility, Weighted Score, and Limiting Factor

Pursuant to Section 6 of the Policy, the Importance and Urgency scores in Resolution 1 are converted to Percentile Rank and evaluated against the Eligibility Threshold. Pursuant to Section 5.4, each eligible Domain is classified by Limiting Factor:

| Domain | Importance %ile | Urgency %ile | Eligible (I% ≥ 50) | Limiting Factor | Weighted Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fundraising | 100.0 | 20.0 | Yes | Money | 120.0 |
| Outreach | 80.0 | 70.0 | Yes | Time | 136.0 |
| Lobbying | 40.0 | 40.0 | No | — | — |
| Research | 60.0 | 70.0 | Yes | Time | 102.0 |
| Deployment | 20.0 | 100.0 | No | — | — |
| Capital & Partnership Facilitation | 0.0 | 0.0 | No | — | — |

**Basis for Limiting Factor.** Fundraising's activities (testing donor, capital-leverage, and consultancy approaches) depend on having real capital to test with, while the labor hours involved are comparatively fixed regardless of funding level: Money-Limited. Outreach and Research both depend on volunteer production and analysis hours to produce more or better output, while their capital requirements are comparatively fixed and modest: Time-Limited.

## Resolution 3: Money Pool, Time Pool, and Selection

The Board hereby sets the Corporation's total annual budget at $2,280.00. Of that amount, $453.55 is committed to the Corporation's baseline recurring operating expenses under Resolution 15 of the Written Consent dated June 23, 2026 (registered agent, operational infrastructure, domain registrations, and Google Workspace, together with a 10% contingency reserve), leaving a Domain-level Money Pool of **$1,826.45**. The Board further sets the Domain-level Time Pool at **1,000 hours** for the year.

Pursuant to Section 7 of the Policy, every Domain meeting the Eligibility Threshold in Resolution 2 is selected for resourcing: **Fundraising, Outreach, and Research.** Lobbying, Deployment, and Capital & Partnership Facilitation are not selected, having failed to meet the Eligibility Threshold.

## Resolution 5: Project-Level Rounds Within Selected Domains

Pursuant to Section 3.2 of the Policy, a separate Project-level round is conducted within each Domain selected under Resolution 3, applying Sections 4 through 9 of the Policy to Project Options competing within that Domain, using that Domain's own Money Allocation and Time Allocation from Resolution 4 as its Money Pool and Time Pool.

### Outreach — Money Pool $300.16, Time Pool 442.4 hours

**Project: YouTube channel producing at least monthly videos**

(a)–(c) As established in the Outreach domain review above. (d) Leverage: video is the specific format in which independent creators currently hold the strongest measured trust advantage over legacy outlets, per the domain's evidence. (e) Why this project: a monthly cadence is the minimum publication frequency capable of building and holding an audience. (f) Outcome: a channel with a regular publication record and a measurable subscriber base. (g) Metrics: videos published per period, view count, subscriber growth, watch-through rate. (h) Harms: an unmet monthly cadence damages audience trust more than never having committed to one. (i) Risky assumption: that a monthly cadence is achievable within this project's allocation. (j) Revise trigger: cadence is missed for two consecutive periods.

**Project: Social media microblog accounts curating underrepresented news and research**

(a)–(c) As established in the Outreach domain review above. (d) Leverage: short-form curation reaches audiences the channel's longer-form format does not, at lower per-item production cost. (e) Why this project: curating underrepresented material is a distinct activity from original video production. (f) Outcome: a regularly updated microblog presence surfacing underrepresented news and research. (g) Metrics: posting frequency, engagement per post, follower growth. (h) Harms: curation without original verification risks amplifying source material against the Editorial and Compliance Policy's sourcing standard. (i) Risky assumption: that curated material can be verified to that standard within this project's allocation. (j) Revise trigger: a correction is required under the Editorial and Compliance Policy at a rate exceeding a defined threshold.

**Project: Local event outreach to engage aligned activists**

(a)–(c) As established in the Outreach domain review above. (d) Leverage: in-person engagement reaches an audience segment online content alone does not reach. (e) Why this project: it converts existing audience interest into direct participation. (f) Outcome: a measurable count of events attended or hosted and participants engaged. (g) Metrics: events conducted, attendance, participant follow-through. (h) Harms: event costs and travel time are harder to bound in advance than content-production costs. (i) Risky assumption: that event costs can be estimated reliably enough to stay within this project's allocation. (j) Revise trigger: actual per-event cost exceeds the estimate on which this project's allocation was based.

**Project: Online marketing campaign to boost visibility**

(a)–(c) As established in the Outreach domain review above. (d) Leverage: paid promotion can extend audience reach beyond organic publication alone. (e) Why this project: it is the domain's most direct lever for audience growth, independent of content production itself. (f) Outcome: measurable growth in audience reach attributable to the campaign. (g) Metrics: cost per acquired viewer or follower, reach, conversion to subscriber or follower status. (h) Harms: paid acquisition that does not convert to durable engagement consumes resources without advancing the domain's education purpose. (i) Risky assumption: that paid acquisition converts to durable engagement rather than one-time impressions. (j) Revise trigger: cost per durably retained viewer or follower exceeds a defined threshold.

### Research — Money Pool $225.12, Time Pool 331.8 hours

**Project: Meta-analyses building a holistic picture of the most effective means to realize the mission**

(a)–(c) As established in the Research domain review above. (d) Leverage: a holistic view across the conditions named in the Corporation's purposes strengthens every other domain's evidentiary basis. (e) Why this project: it is the direct execution of this domain's exempt research purpose. (f) Outcome: a body of meta-analysis covering the conditions named in the Corporation's purposes. (g) Metrics: meta-analyses completed, claim density and defensibility. (h) Harms: a scope spanning multiple conditions at once risks shallower treatment of any one than a narrower analysis would achieve. (i) Risky assumption: that a holistic scope is achievable within this project's allocation without sacrificing rigor. (j) Revise trigger: correction rate on this project's claims exceeds a defined threshold.

**Project: Workflow for discovery and publication of underrepresented research**

(a)–(c) As established in the Research domain review above. (d) Leverage: a repeatable workflow reduces the marginal cost of each future publication compared to an ad hoc process. (e) Why this project: it builds durable capacity for this domain rather than a single output. (f) Outcome: a documented, repeatable process for discovering and publishing underrepresented research. (g) Metrics: publications produced through the workflow, time per publication. (h) Harms: a workflow optimized for speed carries the same rigor exposure identified in the domain review. (i) Risky assumption: that a repeatable workflow meets the domain's evidentiary standard as reliably as a case-by-case process. (j) Revise trigger: workflow-produced publications show a correction rate exceeding a defined threshold.

**Project: System for documenting, classifying, and tracking federal actions out of alignment with the mission**

(a)–(c) As established in the Research domain review above. (d) Leverage: federal regulatory and legislative data is currently fragmented across many separate sources; consolidating it into one system creates a resource other organizations do not maintain. (e) Why this project: it is a direct execution of this domain's exempt research purpose with respect to the governance-accountability systems named in the Corporation's purposes. (f) Outcome: a maintained system documenting, classifying, and tracking relevant federal actions. (g) Metrics: actions tracked and classified, system currency. (h) Harms: classifying a government action as mission-misaligned is itself a judgment call carrying the same rigor exposure identified in the domain review, at higher volume than a single analysis. (i) Risky assumption: that ongoing maintenance of the system can be sustained within this project's allocation. (j) Revise trigger: the system falls out of date relative to new federal actions for a defined period.

**Project: Review of pathways to implementing research conclusions as actionable future projects**

(a)–(c) As established in the Research domain review above. (d) Leverage: this domain's output advances the Corporation's purposes fully once it connects to an executable project. (e) Why this project: it is the direct link between this domain's output and the other domains' Project-level rounds under Section 3.2 of the Policy. (f) Outcome: a documented set of pathways connecting specific research conclusions to specific potential future projects. (g) Metrics: pathways documented, and the rate at which they are subsequently proposed as Project Options in future rounds. (h) Harms: a review disconnected from actual future rounds produces documentation without effect. (i) Risky assumption: that documented pathways are in fact proposed as Options in future Project-level rounds. (j) Revise trigger: no pathway from this review is proposed as an Option within a defined number of subsequent rounds.
